# Rewrite patterns

Worked pairs for each category, plus the judgment calls. The point of reading
these is calibration: seeing how far to move, and where to stop.

---

## Inflated significance

**Before:** Our partnership with Meridian stands as a testament to our
commitment to quality, playing a pivotal role in how we serve the region.

**After:** We build on Meridian hardware because it is the only platform in
this price bracket we can get spare parts for in under a week.

The rewrite is shorter, makes a checkable claim, and gives the reader a reason
instead of an adjective. That is the trade in every one of these.

**Before:** The 2019 refit marked a watershed moment in the building's history,
leaving a lasting impact on the community it serves.

**After:** The 2019 refit added the second staircase, which is why the upper
floor could be licensed for public use.

---

## Superficial analysis tails

**Before:** The unit switches over in under 20 milliseconds, ensuring
uninterrupted operation and highlighting the system's reliability.

**After:** The unit switches over in under 20 milliseconds. Your router does
not reboot.

Delete the tail. If it deserved a sentence, give it one, and make it concrete
enough to be wrong.

**Before:** Prices are published openly, reflecting a broader industry shift
toward transparency.

**After:** Prices are published openly. Most installers here quote on request
only, so this is unusual, and it is deliberate.

---

## Negative parallelism

**Before:** This isn't just a battery. It's peace of mind.

**After:** The battery runs the house for about nine hours on essentials.

**Before:** We don't sell watts. We sell hours.

**After:** Panels are rated in kilowatts, which tells you how fast the system
makes power at noon. The number that decides your evening is the battery's
kilowatt-hours. That is what our systems are named after.

The second rewrite is longer, and that is correct. The slogan compressed a real
idea into a shape that reads as advertising. Unpacking it makes the idea
survive and the advertising go away.

**Keep it when the contrast is the information:**

> Ask whether the figure quoted is total capacity or usable capacity.

That is not negative parallelism. The two things are genuinely different and
the difference is the reader's problem. Leave it.

## Rule of three

**Before:** Certified crews, honest pricing, and service that answers.

**After:** Our crews are TESDA-certified, and we publish the prices. Whether we
answer the phone in year four is the part you should actually check.

**Before:** Fast, reliable, and built to last.

**After:** It has run for six years without a service call.

**Keep it when there are three of them:**

> We work in Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur, La Union and Pangasinan.

Four provinces, named because a reader needs to know if they are in one. Not a
tell. Do not turn a real list into prose to satisfy a checklist.

## Vague attribution

**Before:** Experts recommend sizing storage with headroom, and studies show
most households underestimate their evening load.

**After:** We size with about 20% headroom. Left to their own arithmetic, most
people we assess underestimate the evening load, usually because they forget
the fridge cycles all night.

If a real source exists, name it. If it does not, own the claim as your own
observation or drop it.

## Hedging

**Before:** A battery-only system may potentially offer some benefits for
certain condominium residents, though results may vary.

**After:** If you rent, a battery on its own is usually the right buy. It will
not lower your bill, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling.

Taking the position is the humanizing move. Note the second sentence keeps a
real limitation. Removing hedging does not mean removing honesty; it means
replacing vague hedging with specific limits.

---

## Hollow wrap-ups

**Before:** In conclusion, choosing the right system requires careful
consideration of your unique needs and circumstances.

**After:** [delete]

Then check whether the section needs a real ending. Often the last substantive
paragraph was already the ending, and the summary was covering for the writer
not knowing how to stop.

---

## Metronome rhythm

**Before:**
> The system charges during daylight hours. The battery stores excess energy
> for later use. At night the stored energy powers the home. This cycle repeats
> each day without intervention.

Four sentences, 8–10 words each, identical shape. Nothing is wrong with any
one of them, which is the problem.

**After:**
> The roof runs the house all day and puts what is left into the battery. At
> night the battery takes over. Nobody does anything.

Three sentences of 17, 7 and 3 words. Same information. The rhythm now has a
shape, and the short last sentence carries the actual selling point.

Watch the failure mode on the other side:

> The roof runs the house. All day. Then the battery. Every night. No effort.
> Ever.

That is over-correction. It reads as someone performing humanity.

## Em dashes

**Before:** The assessment measures your roof — the shading, the structure, the
panel — and your consumption history — twelve months of it — before anyone
quotes a number.

**After:** The assessment measures your roof: shading, structure, panel. It
also needs twelve months of bills. Nobody quotes a number before that.

Three em dash pairs in one sentence is the tell. One em dash in a page, used
where a dash genuinely fits, is not, and stripping it out makes the writing
worse.

---

## Bolded lead-ins

**Before:**
> - **Scalability:** the system scales easily as your needs grow.
> - **Reliability:** you can rely on it to keep running.

**After:**
> - Add 15 kWh at a time, up to 90 kWh, without rewiring.
> - Six years in the field, no service calls on the inverter.

The bolded-term-then-restatement shape is close to nonexistent in natural
writing. Either the bold is doing nothing, or the sentence is.

## Assistant chatter

**Before:** Certainly! Here's a comprehensive overview of your options. I hope
this helps — let me know if you'd like me to adjust anything!

**After:** [delete entirely]

---

## Promotional adjective stacking

**Before:** Our state-of-the-art, world-class solution delivers seamless,
robust performance for the modern home.

**After:** One sealed outdoor unit. It is rated IP65, so it lives outside in
the rain, and it runs under 30 dB.

Every adjective in the first version is unfalsifiable. Every claim in the
second can be checked, which is what makes it read as written by someone who
knows the product.

---

## The judgment table

Run each candidate through this before touching it.

| Ask | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Does deleting this lose information? | Keep it | It was decoration — cut |
| Is this how the author actually talks? | Keep it | Consider rewriting |
| Could a reader check this claim? | Probably fine | Make it checkable or cut it |
| Is the pattern here once, or on every page? | Once is style | Every page is a tic |
| Would fixing this make the sentence worse? | Leave it | Fix it |

The last row overrides everything above it.
